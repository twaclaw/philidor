# First Back-game (two Pawns against one)

*On the 1st move of the Black.*

1. **White.** The king, at his queen's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's fourth square.

2. **White.** The king, at his third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his third square.

   > Had he played his king to your king's knight's fourth square, he would likewise lose the game.

3. **White.** The king, at his fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's third square.

   > Had he played his king to his second square, or to his queen's second square, you must have advanced your king opposite to his, in order to effect the taking of his pawn.

4. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The pawn takes the pawn.

   > He could have declined taking your pawn; but that other way of playing will be found in our next back-game.

5. **White.** The king retakes the pawn.
   **Black.** The king, at his second square.

6. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

7. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's rook's second square, and will win.

   > As often as the king is before a pawn, the adversary cannot hinder you from making a queen.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 1.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
