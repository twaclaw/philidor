# A Drawn-game of a Knight far off from his King, against a Pawn near making a Queen

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at his rook's second square.
- The knight, at his queen's rook's second square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at the adverse queen's knight's third square.
- The pawn, at the adverse queen's rook's third square.

1. **White.** The knight gives check, at his queen's bishop's square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's knight's second square.

2. **White.** The knight gives check, at his queen's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

3. **White.** The knight, at his queen's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's knight's third square.

4. **White.** The knight, at his queen's third square.

   > He cannot push his pawn without receiving a double check from your knight; therefore it is a drawn-game. It is necessary to observe, that in such positions, when the knight cannot give check, nor hinder the pawn from going forwards, a double check must necessarily happen at the second move. A DRAWN-GAME, Or a PARTY won, with two PAWNS against one.
