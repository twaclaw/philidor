# Method of giving Check-mate with a Rook and a Bishop against a Rook

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at the adverse king's third square.
- The rook, at its queen's bishop's square.
- The bishop, at the adverse king's fourth square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at his home.
- The rook, at its queen's second square.

1. **White.** The rook gives check.
   **Black.** The rook covers the check.

2. **White.** The rook, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse queen's second square.

   > To give check-mate, you must force your adversary to place his rook either at your queen's square, or at your queen's third square. In either position, the game must be won, after a few computed moves.

3. **White.** The rook, at the adverse queen's knight's second square.

   > He is now obliged to play his rook to your queen's square, or to your queen's third square.

   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse queen's square.

   > You have already forced him to play his rook to one of those squares where he must absolutely lose the game: but this is not enough; for your rook must not be farther from your king than the distance which a knight, by a single regular move, may go. If you were to play your rook to its queen's bishop's second square, he would play his rook to your queen's second square; and you must be eternally beginning afresh: whereas, by passing your rook on your right, he must necessarily, to ward off the check-mate, play his rook to your king's bishop's square, which is no better than that of his queen.

4. **White.** The rook, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's bishop's square.

   > As he might have played his king to his bishop's square, we will make it the subject of a back-game.

5. **White.** The bishop, at his king's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

   > He could have played his rook to your king's bishop's third square. The subject of another back-game.

6. **White.** The rook, at its king's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his home.

   > He brings back his king, so that he may be in readiness to cover the check of your rook.

7. **White.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse queen's square.

   > He might have played the king to his bishop's square. The subject of the last back-game.

8. **White.** The bishop, at his king's rook's fourth square
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

9. **White.** The bishop, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The rook gives check, at the adverse king's square.

10. **White.** The bishop covers the check.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

11. **White.** The rook, at its king's rook's fourth square, and must win.
