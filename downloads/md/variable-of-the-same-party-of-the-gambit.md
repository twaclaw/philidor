# Variable of the same Party of the Gambit

*On the 5th move of the Black.*

1. **White.** The king's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The same.

2. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the pawn.

3. **White.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, two moves.

4. **White.** The king's rook's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

5. **White.** The king's knight, at the adverse king's fourth square.
   **Black.** The queen, at her king's second square.

6. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

7. **White.** The king's knight takes the pawn.
   **Black.** The queen takes the king's pawn, and gives check.

8. **White.** The queen covers the check.

   > In this situation you must change the queens, as nothing better can be done, and afterwards take the gambit's pawn. The two games are even. It is very easily seen, that this way of playing the gambit is neither of a very interesting nature, nor affords the same after-games; and that a number of variations sprung from Salvio's gambit.


   > In the former edition, (of 1749) I appointed this situation as the best for the black, and, besides, the only good one to make a proper defence: but, as many lovers of this game desire to know the means of forcing the black to the present position, some instructions agreeable to it will be given in my subsequent annotations.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 1.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
