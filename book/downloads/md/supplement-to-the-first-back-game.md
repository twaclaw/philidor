# Supplement to the First Back-game

*On the 4th move of the Black.*

4. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his queen's knight's third square.

5. **White.** The king's knight, at his king's second square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.

6. **White.** The queen, at her third square.
   **Black.** The king castles.

7. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the queen's pawn.

8. **White.** The king's pawn, one move.

   > If, on that move, he withdraws his king's knight, he gives you an opportunity of establishing your pawns in the centre.

   **Black.** The queen's pawn, two moves.

9. **White.** The king's bishop, at his queen's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at the adverse king's fourth square.

10. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn takes the pawn.
   **Black.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.

   > He pushes this pawn two moves, in order to enable himself to place his queen's bishop at his king's second square.

11. **White.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.

   > In this situation the black have as good a game as the white. There is, indeed, a pawn passed and sustained on the side of the white; but this advantage is quite counterbalanced by the post of the royal knight of the black, who cannot be diflodged but by changing piece for piece; and in that case, the pawns of the black would re-unite in the centre, giving, equally to them as to the white, a pafs'd pawn. Mij 164]
