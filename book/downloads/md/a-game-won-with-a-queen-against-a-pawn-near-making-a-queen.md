# A Game won with a Queen against a Pawn near making a Queen

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at the adverse queen's knight's second square.
- The queen, at the adverse queen's bishop's square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.
- The pawn, at the adverse king's second square.

1. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.

   > To win this game, the queen must be previously brought, as near as possible, to the adverse king.

   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's second square.

2. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.

3. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's second square.

4. **White.** The queen, at her king's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's square.

   > It is this move which enables you to win the game, because you force him to play his king under his pawn.

5. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's square.

   > As his pawn cannot make a queen, you must make a good use of this instant to bring your king next to his.

6. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's second square.

7. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse queen's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

8. **White.** The queen gives check, at her bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's second square.

9. **White.** The queen gives check, at her fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

10. **White.** The queen, at her king's third square.

   > It is always the same move, which forces him to play his king under his pawn.

   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's square.

11. **White.** The queen gives check, at her third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's square.

12. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's fourth square.

   > You will proceed with the same moves till your king comes next to his pawn, and then the game is won.
