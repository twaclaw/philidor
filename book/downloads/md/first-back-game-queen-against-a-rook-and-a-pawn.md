# First Back-game (Queen against a Rook and a Pawn)

*On the 9th move of the Black.*

9. **White.** The queen, at the adverse queen's bishop's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its king's rook's fourth square.

   > His rook being far off from its king, you ought to take her by a double check, or at least to take his pawn.

10. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse queen's rook's square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's fourth square.

11. **White.** The queen gives check, at her rook's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's third square.

12. **White.** The queen gives check, at her rook's third square.
   **Black.** The king where he pleases, his pawn being lost.

   > The subject of this back-game is to prove how easy it is, with a queen, to force by a double check, a rook, when at a distance from its king.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 9.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
