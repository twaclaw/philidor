# A Drawn-game with a single Pawn

*Or a game won, if he who remains with his King alone does not play well.*

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at his bishop's fourth square.
- The pawn, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at his bishop's third square.

1. **White.** The king, at his fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's second square.

   > It is always necessary to bring his king to the face of the adverse pawn, so that his king may oppose the adverse king.

2. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his second square.

3. **White.** The pawn, one move, giving check.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's second square.

4. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

   > If he had removed his king to his home, or to his king's knight's square, he would have lost the game. The subject of a backgame.

5. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his home.

   > Were you in the present position to push your pawn, you could not sustain it without making a stale-mate.

6. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's second square.

   > His defence depends upon opposing his king to yours; for if, on the contrary, you could oppose your king to his, you would win the game.

7. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

   > He always brings his king to the face of yours, which infallibly bring's on a drawngame.

8. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

9. **White.** The pawn, one move, giving check.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

10. **White.** The king is forced to leave the pawn.

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
