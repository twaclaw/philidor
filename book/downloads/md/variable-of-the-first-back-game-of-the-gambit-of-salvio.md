# Variable of the First Back-game of the Gambit of Salvio

*On the 7th move of the Black.*

7. **White.** The king's bishop takes the pawn, and gives check.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's square, instead of his own second square.

   > You lie no more under the necessity of withdrawing your bishop, as in the first backgame: fo that, being placed at this square, he may hinder the adverse king's knight from posting himself at his king's rook's fourth square: a dangerous move.

8. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.

   > Had you withdrawn your king's bishop to give double check with your knight, he would then have played his knight to his king's rook's fourth square, and sacrificed his rook to win the game.

   **Black.** The king's or gambit's pawn, one move.

   > He might have played this move in two other different ways: the first is, to push his queen's pawn upon your knight, to force him to retreat; the other, to take your king's pawn with his kright: in this case you should offer to change the queens, by playing yours to your king's square.

9. **White.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > Had you taken his pawn with yours, he would have won the game.

   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

10. **White.** The king's knight, at his queen's third square.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the knight's pawn.

11. **White.** The king retakes the pawn.
   **Black.** The queen gives check.

12. **White.** The king, at his knight's square,

   > If you had removed the king to his bishop's second square, he would have won the game, by giving check with his king's knight's pawn, and at the next move another check with his king's knight.

   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

13. **White.** The king's knight, at his bishop's fourth square. ()
