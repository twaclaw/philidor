# First Back-game of the Method of Playing

*Beginning at the 3d move of the Black.*

3. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > He plays this knight, to hinder you from pushing your queen's pawn two moves. If, instead of this knight, he had played his queen's bishop's pawn one move, your next move might equally take place.

4. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the pawn.

   > He could have prevented this move, by removing the bishop to his queen's knight's third square. This will make a supplement to this back-game.

5. **White.** The king's bishop takes the king's bishop's pawn.
   **Black.** The king takes the bishop.

   > If he does not take your bishop, you must take his king's knight, and push your queen's knight's pawn two steps, attacking his bishop; and afterwards push upon his queen's knight, in order to retake the pawn with your queen's bishop's pawn. SUPPLEMENT

6. **White.** The queen, at the adverse king's rook's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

7. **White.** The queen takes the king's bishop, and will have a very good game.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 3.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
