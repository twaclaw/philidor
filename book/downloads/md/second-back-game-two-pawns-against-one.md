# Second Back-game (two Pawns against one)

*On the 2d move of the Black.*

1. **White.** The king, at his queen's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's fourth square.

2. **White.** The king, at his third square.

   > You again get the move over him, because he cannot play his king fronting yours: he must absolutely retrograde either to his third square, or to his queen's third square. In either case, your king may place himself in opposition to his.

   **Black.** The king, at his third square.

3. **White.** The king, at his fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's third square.

   > If he had withdrawn his king to his second square, or to his queen's second square, you must have advanced your king opposite to his.

4. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king, at his second square.

5. **White.** The pawn, one move, giving check.

   > Had you taken his pawn with yours, it would have been a drawn-game.

   **Black.** The king, at his third square.

6. **White.** The king, at his queen's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's third square.

   > In this position he has got the move over you, since he may oppose your king, but by the sacrifice of a pawn you may recover it over him, and a single pawn is enough to win the game.

7. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king, at his second square.

8. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king takes the pawn.

9. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

10. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's second square.

11. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

12. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's second square.

13. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's square.

14. **White.** The king takes the pawn.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

15. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

16. **White.** The pawn, one move, and makes a queen without any opposition.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 1.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
