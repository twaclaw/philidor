# Second Back-game of the Second Party

*Beginning from the 8th move of the Black.*

8. **White.** The queen, at her king's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.

9. **White.** The king's bishop, at his king's second square.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

10. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

11. **White.** The king's bishop, at his third square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop, at his queen's fourth square.

12. **White.** The queen's knight's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's knight's pawn, two steps.

13. **White.** The queen's rook's pawn, two steps.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn takes the pawn.

14. **White.** The queen's rook's pawn retakes the pawn.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop takes the adversary's bishop.

15. **White.** The king's knight retakes the bishop.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his queen's second square.

16. **White.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's knight's square.

17. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his third square.

18. **White.** The queen's knight, at his queen's second square.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at the adversary's queen's knight's fourth square.

19. **White.** The king castles, and will undoubtedly win the game; because all his pawns situated on his right side are well sustained; whilst his adversary's two pawns, being separated, are likely to be lost.

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 8.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
