# Back-game (a Drawn-game with a single Pawn)

*On the 4th move of the Black.*

4. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his home, or at his royal knight's square.

5. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

6. **White.** The pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king, at his royal knight's second square.

7. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's second square, and will win.

**Notes with no reference in the text.** The scan lost the mark that called these, so no move points to them.

> It may be seen by these examples, that whoever acts defensively must always bring his king over-against his adversary's pawn.

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 4.*
