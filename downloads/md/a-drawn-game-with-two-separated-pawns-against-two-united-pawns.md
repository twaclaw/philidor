# A Drawn-game with two separated Pawns against two united Pawns

1. **White.** The king, at his third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his fourth square.

2. **White.** The king, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's fourth square.

3. **White.** The king, at his knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his fourth square.

   > In this position, if the black were to play first, the white would win the game.

4. **White.** The king, at his knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's third square.

5. **White.** The king, at his knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his fourth square.

   > Had he played his king to his bishop's fourth square, you would have got again the the move over him; which would have given you the game.

6. **White.** The king, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's fourth square.

   > As he don't push his pawn, nor give you leave to take the place opposite to his king, it must be a drawn-game.
