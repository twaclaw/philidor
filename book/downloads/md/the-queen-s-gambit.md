# The Queen's Gambit

*Otherwise the Gambit of Aleppo. With six back-games.*

1. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The same.

2. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The pawn takes the pawn.

3. **White.** The king's pawn, two moves.

   > If, instead of two, you had pushed this pawn but one move, your adversary would have shut up your queen's bishop for half the game. The subject of a back-game.

   **Black.** The king's pawn, two moves.

   > If, instead of playing this pawn, he had sustained the gambit's pawn, he had lost the game; as will be seen by a back-game: but, if he had neither pushed this pawn, nor taken the gambit's pawn, in this case you must have pushed your king's bishop's pawn two moves, in order to have three pawns in front.

4. **White.** The queen's pawn, one move.

   > If, instead of pushing this pawn forwards, you had taken his king's pawn, you had lost the advantage of the attack. The subject of a back-game.

   **Black.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.

   > If he had played any thing else, you must have pushed your king's bishop's pawn two moves: by this means you would have procured for your pieces an entire liberty to act.

5. **White.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.

6. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his queen's bishop's fourth square, Ath

7. **White.** The queen's knight, at his rook's third square.
   **Black.** The bishop takes the adversary's king's knight.

   > If, instead of taking your knight, he had played his bishop to your queen's fourth square, you might have attacked him with your king's knight, and taken him the subsequent move.

8. **White.** The rook retakes the bishop.
   **Black.** The king castles.

   > If he had pushed his queen's knight's pawn two moves, in order to sustain the gambit's pawn, he had lost the game, (the subject of a back-game): and if, instead of either of these two moves, he had chosen to take your king's pawn, your retaking it would have hindered him from taking your's again with his knight; because he would have lost the game by your giving him check afterwards with your queen.

9. **White.** The knight, at his queen's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The pawn takes the pawn.

10. **White.** The king's bishop takes the gambit's pawn.

   > This particular move requires a backgame. If you had retaken his king's bishop's pawn with your king's bishop's pawn, you had lost the game again.

   **Black.** The pawn takes the adverse king's bishop's pawn.

11. **White.** The pawn retakes the pawn.

   > In retaking with this pawn, you give an opening to your rook upon his king.

   **Black.** The queen's bishop, at his king's bishop's fourth square.

12. **White.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his queen's second square.

13. **White.** The queen, at her second square.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his third square.

14. **White.** The queen's bishop takes the knight.
   **Black.** The rook's pawn retakes the bishop.

15. **White.** The king castles on his queen's side.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's square.

   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

   **Black.** The queen, at her third square.
