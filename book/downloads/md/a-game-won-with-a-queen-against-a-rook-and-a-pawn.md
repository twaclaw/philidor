# A Game won with a Queen against a Rook and a Pawn

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at his bishop's fourth square.
- The queen, at her third square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at his second square.
- The queen's pawn, at its third square.
- The rook, at its king's fourth square. (a)

1. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's rook's second square.

   > His defence entirely depends upon not letting your king pass, and afterwards playing his rook alternately from its queen's bishop's fourth square to its king's fourth square, being always supported by his pawn.

   **Black.** The king, at his third square.

2. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's knight's square.

   > This move is needless; and I point it out only to shew by what means turns may be got or lost.

   **Black.** The king, at his second square.

3. **White.** The queen, at the adverse queen's bishop's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's fourth square.

4. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse queen's knight's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his third square.

5. **White.** The queen, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its king's fourth square.

6. **White.** The queen, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

   > This move determines the winning of the game: you must then, first of all, take this position.

   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's fourth square.

7. **White.** The queen, at the adverse queen's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its king's fourth square.

8. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's square.

   > By this check you force his king to go towards his pawn. This move will make the passage of your king more easy afterwards.

   **Black.** The king, at his queen's fourth square.

9. **White.** The queen, at the adverse queen's bishop's square.

   > You put him under the necessity of removing his rook from its king, or giving a free passage to your king.

   **Black.** The rook gives check.

   > If he had played his king to your queen's fourth square, you must have played your queen to his queen's bishop's third square: but he might also have played his rook to his king's rook's fourth square, to hinder the passage of your king; the subject of a back-game. $ iv

10. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook gives check.

11. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's fourth square.

   > If he had played his king to your queen's fourth square, you must have played your queen to his queen's bishop's third square.

12. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook covers the check.

13. **White.** The queen gives check, at her third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's bishop's fourth square.

14. **White.** The queen, at her second square.

   > At the instant your king gets behind his pawn, the advantage of the game will turn on your side: but to attain this, you must force him to play his king.

   **Black.** The king, at his queen's bishop's third square.

15. **White.** The queen, at her fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's second square.

   > As he could have played his king to his queen's bishop's second square. Another subject of a back-game.

16. **White.** The queen, at her bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's fourth square.

   > If he had put away his rook from its king, you must have endeavoured to find some way of forcing his rook by a double check.

17. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's bishop's third square.

18. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's second square.
   **Black.** The rook gives check.

19. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's fourth square.

20. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse queen's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's fourth square.

21. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's bishop's third square. 22.16

22. **White.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse queen's bishop's fourth square.

23. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queer's bishop's fourth square.

24. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's 2.1 fourth square, and must win.

   > As many lovers of this game may not be acquainted with the method of giving check-mate, with a queen against a rook, we will end this party by way of back-game.
