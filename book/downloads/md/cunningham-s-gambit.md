# Cunningham's Gambit

*With two back-games; one from the 7th, and the other at the 11th move.*

1. **White.** The king's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The same.

2. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the pawn.

3. **White.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his king's second square.

4. **White.** The king's bishop, at his queen's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king's bishop gives check.

5. **White.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The pawn takes the pawn.

6. **White.** The king castles.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the adverse king's rook's pawn.

7. **White.** The king, at his rook's square.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his third square.

   > If, instead of playing this bishop to his third square, he had played him to his king's second square, you would have won the game in a few moves, which you will fee by a back-game.

8. **White.** The king's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, two moves.

   > If he did not sacrifice his bishop, you would undoubtedly win the game; but losing him, and preserving three pawns for that piece, he must become your conqueror by the strength of his pawns, provided he is not too hasty in pushing them forwards, before he has got out all his pieces.

9. **White.** The king's pawn takes the bishop.
   **Black.** The king's knight takes the pawn.

10. **White.** The king's bishop, at his queen's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.

11. **White.** The queen's pawn, one move.

   > If you had pushed this pawn two moves, you had given his knight a free entry into your game, which would have lost you the party: the subject of a back-game.

   **Black.** The king's rook's pawn, one move.

   > This move is of great consequence to him for ensuring the party, because it hin ders you from attacking his king's knight with your queen's bishop, which would have enabled you to separate his pawns, by sacrificing a rook for one of his knights, which would have turned the advantage of the game on your side.

12. **White.** The queen's bishop, at his king's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn, two moves.

13. **White.** The queen's bishop takes the pawn next to his king.
   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.

14. **White.** The queen's knight, at his queen's second square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at the adverse king's knight's fourth square.

   > He plays this knight to take your queen's bishop, who would prove incommodious to him, in case he should castle on his queen's side. It is here proper to observe, as a general rule, that, when either has advanced pawns, the best way is to strive to get rid of the adverse bishops, because they can oppose the pawns, and stop their way much better than any other piece.

15. **White.** The queen, at her king's second square.

   > Not knowing how to save your bishop, you play your queen, to assume his place again when taken.

   **Black.** The knight takes the bishop.

16. **White.** The queen takes the knight.
   **Black.** The queen, at her knight's square.

   > He offers to change his queen with yours, to break the direction of your queen; so that he may place her at her third square, in case you refuse to take her.

17. **White.** The queen takes the queen.

   > If you did not take the queen, your game would be still in a worse state. I ij [116 1

   **Black.** The rook retakes the queen.

18. **White.** The queen's rook, at its king's square.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's second square.

19. **White.** The king's knight gives check.
   **Black.** The knight takes the knight.

20. **White.** The queen's rook retakes the knight.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's third square.

21. **White.** The king's rook, at its king's square.
   **Black.** The queen's knight's pawn, two moves.

22. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's rook, at its king's square.

23. **White.** The queen's rook's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The queen's rook's pawn, one move.

24. **White.** The knight, at his king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, two moves.

25. **White.** The king, at his knight's second square.
   **Black.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.

   > If he had pushed this pawn two moves, you had gained his queen's pawn, taking it with your bishop: this would have mended your game very much.

26. **White.** The queen's rook, at its king's second square.
   **Black.** The king's rook's pawn, one move.

27. **White.** The queen's rook's pawn takes the pawn.
   **Black.** The pawn retakes the pawn.

28. **White.** The king'srook, at its queen's rook's square.
   **Black.** The queen's rook, at her home.

   > It is an object, always to keep the pafsages, and hinder the adversary from doubling his rooks; therefore he proposes to change piece for piece.

29. **White.** The king's rook returns to its king's square.
   **Black.** The bishop, at his queen's second square.

30. **White.** The queen's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.

31. **White.** The bishop, at his queen's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The king's rook's pawn, one move.

   > He plays this pawn, in order afterwards to push that of his king's knight upon your knight, with an intention to force him from his post; but if he had pushed his knight's pawn before he played this, your knight, posted at your king's rook's fourth square, would have stopped the progress of all his pawns.

32. **White.** The king's rook, at his home.
   **Black.** The king's rook, at its fourth square.

   > If, instead of playing this, he had given check with his rook's pawn, he would have played entirely against the instruction given in the first Party. Vide reflexion.

33. **White.** The queen's knight's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen's rook, at its king's rook's square.

34. **White.** The queen's knight's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

35. **White.** The knight, at his queen's second square.
   **Black.** The king's rook, at its king's knight's fourth square.

36. **White.** The king's rook, at its king's bishop's square.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

37. **White.** The rook takes the pawn, and gives check.
   **Black.** The king, at his queen's bishop's second square.

38. **White.** The king's rook, at the adverse king's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king's rook's pawn, one move, and gives check.

39. **White.** The king, at his knight's square.
   **Black.** The king's knight's pawn, one move.

40. **White.** The rook takes the rook.
   **Black.** The rook's pawn gives check.

41. **White.** The king takes the knight's pawn.
   **Black.** The rook's pawn makes a queen, and gives check.

42. **White.** The king, at his bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The rook gives check, at its king's bishop's square.

43. **White.** The king, at his third square.
   **Black.** The queen gives check at the adverse king's rook's third square.

44. **White.** The knight covers the check, having no other way.
   **Black.** The queen takes the knight, and afterwards the rook, and gives mate in two moves after.
