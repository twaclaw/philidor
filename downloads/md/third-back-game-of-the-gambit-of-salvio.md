# Third Back-game of the Gambit of Salvio

*On the 8th move of the Black.*

8. **White.** The king takes the queen.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

   > If, instead of this move, he had played his queen's knight to his bishop's third square, you should then have taken his knight with yours, and sustained your royal pawn, by pushing that of your queen one move.

9. **White.** The knight takes the king's bishop's pawn.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

10. **White.** The king's bishop takes the queen's pawn.

   > It is better to take his pawn, than to give him leave to take your bishop; because his knight remains yet in the lurch.

   **Black.** The king's knight takes the bishop.

11. **White.** The king's knight takes the rook.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third

12. **White.** The queen's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his knight's second square.

13. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.

   > This move is absolutely necessary, because he could have taken your king's pawn, by sacrificing his knight, and afterwards taken your queen's knight's pawn with his bishop.

   **Black.** The king's bishop takes the knight.

   > He could have sustained the king's or gambit's pawn, but for no more than two or three moves.

14. **White.** The queen's bishop takes the pawn.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.

15. **White.** The knight, at his queen's second square.

   > In this situation, it is better to advance the king under the pawns, than to castle; and then you will have a better game than your adversary.
