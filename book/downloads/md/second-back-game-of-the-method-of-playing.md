# Second Back-game of the Method of Playing

*On the 4th move of the White.*

4. **White.** The king's knight, at his king's second square.

   > If a pawn were not lost by this move, this place would undoubtedly be the most convenient for the knight, because it does not obstruct the passage of the pawns.

   **Black.** The king's bishop takes the pawn, and gives check.

5. **White.** The king takes the bishop.
   **Black.** The queen, at her bishop's fourth square, giving check.

6. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The queen takes the bishop.

7. **White.** The queen's knight, at its rook's third square.
   **Black.** The queen, at her king's third square.

   > The black must win the game, having the advantage of a pawn, and a good situation besides.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 4.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
