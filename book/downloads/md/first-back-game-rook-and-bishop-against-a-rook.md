# First Back-game (Rook and Bishop against a Rook)

*On the 4th move of the Black.*

4. **White.** The rook, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's square.

5. **White.** The rook, at the adverse king's rook's second square.

   > You force him, by this move, to play his rook to your king's knight's square, to ward off the mate; which gives you the means of taking his rook for nothing.

   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's knight's square.

6. **White.** The rook, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

   > The only way to ward off the mate is, to play his king to his knight's square; for, if he had given you check, you would have preserved all the strength of your attack, by covering with your bishop.

   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

7. **White.** The rook gives check at the adverse queen's bishop's square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's second square.

8. **White.** The rook gives check, at the adverse king's rook's square, and must win.


---

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 4.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
