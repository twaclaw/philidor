# Fourth Variable

*At the 3d move of the Black. (The 1790 edition prints this as the Fifth Regular Party.)*

1. **White.** The king's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The same.

2. **White.** The king's bishop, at his queen's bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The same.

3. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The queen, at her king's bishop's third square.

4. **White.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > As, instead of this move, he might play his queen to your king's rook's fourth square, it will be the subject of a back-game.

   **Black.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > He forces you to get out your king's knight before your pawn: but after the first moves, you must think of the proper means to put it again in action. You might have prevented this move with your queen; but the is better at her home, because, in that place, she enables you to advance, with real force, the same queen's pawn upon his king's bishop.

5. **White.** The queen's knight's pawn, two moves.

   > He plays this knight in order to hinder you from pushing, two moves, your queen's pawn: nevertheless this move may be ventured. A new subject of a back-game.

   **Black.** The king's bishop, at his queen's knight's third square.

6. **White.** The queen's rook's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The queen's rook's pawn, one move.

   > It is not always advantageous to push the pawns of the wings; and they must be advanced cautiously.

7. **White.** The queen's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The same.

8. **White.** The king's rook's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The same.

9. **White.** The queen, at her king's second square.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.

   > If he had advanced this pawn two moves, you must have pushed yours upon his knight, according to the rule already prescribed, always to advance the attacked pawn.

10. **White.** The queen's knight, at his rook's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at his king's second square.

11. **White.** The king's bishop takes the bishop.
   **Black.** The queen retakes the bishop.

   > As he cannot break your king's bishop's direction with his pawns, he strives to do it by the help of his queen's bishop.

12. **White.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king castles with his rook.

   > He might retake this bishop with his pawn, and this would not be badly played.

13. **White.** The queen's knight takes the bishop.
   **Black.** The queen's bishop's pawn takes the knight.

14. **White.** The queen's bishop, at his king's third square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at his queen's bishop's square.

15. **White.** The king castles with his rook.
   **Black.** The king's bishop's pawn, two moves.

16. **White.** The king's pawn takes the pawn.

   > If he had castled on his queen's side, he would have given up to you the advantage of the situation. O ij I 196]

   **Black.** The queen retakes the pawn.

   > Had you not taken this pawn, he would still have advanced it one move upon your bishop to dislodge him, and get an easy attack on your king's wing with his pawns.
