# Another Drawn-game with a Queen against a Pawn near making a Queen

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at his queen's bishop's fourth square.
- The queen, at her fourth square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
- The pawn, at the adverse king's rook's second square.

1. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's second square. 2.

2. **White.** The queen, at her king' 'srook's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's square.

3. **White.** The queen gives check, at her king's knight's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at the adverse king's rook's square.

   > Being forced to take away your queen from that line, to make room for his king, you could never bring up your king in time: so it must be a drawn-game. It is proper to observe, that the pawns of the two bishops, and of the two rooks, at a square of the queen, make a drawn-game, against a queen; and the pawns of the king, the queen, and of the two knights, must lose in such a position, 4.5 1.6
