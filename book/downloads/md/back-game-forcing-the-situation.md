# Back-game (forcing the Situation)

*On the 7th move of the Black.*

7. **White.** The king, at his fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's square.

8. **White.** The bishop, at the adverse king's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook, at its king's bishop's second square.

9. **White.** The rook, at its queen's knight's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's bishop's second square. 10.

10. **White.** The king, at the adverse queen's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his bishop's second square.

11. **White.** The rook gives check.
   **Black.** The king, at his knight's third square.

12. **White.** The rook gives check, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's third square.

13. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's third square
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's second square.

14. **White.** The rook, at its king's knight's square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.

15. **White.** The bishop, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's fourth square.

16. **White.** The rook gives check, at the adverse king's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's third square.

17. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's bishop's second square.
   **Black.** The rook, at the adverse queen's bishop's second square.

18. **White.** The rook, at the adverse queen's knight's fourth square.
   **Black.** The rook gives check.

19. **White.** The bishop covers the check.
   **Black.** The rook, at its queen's second square.

20. **White.** The rook, at the adverse queen's knight's square.
   **Black.** The king, at his rook's fourth square.

21. **White.** The rook, at its queen's knight's fourth square, and will give check at the next move.

   > It may be seen by this back-game, that, when the pieces do not stand in the situation represented here, there are many different ways to force the mate: it is only to be observed, that the bishop must always stand next to his king, to cover him in case of a check.
