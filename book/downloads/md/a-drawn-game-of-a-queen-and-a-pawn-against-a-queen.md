# A Drawn-game of a Queen and a Pawn against a Queen

**Situation of the White**
- The king, at the adverse king's knight's second square.
- The pawn, at the adverse king's bishop's third square.
- The queen, at her third square.
**Situation of the Black**
- The king, at the adverse queen's knight's fourth square.
- The queen, at her bishop's fourth square.

1. **White.** The king's bishop's pawn, one move.

   > In this position, the white ought to look for an opportunity of changing the queen against the queen; and the black, to prevent that exchange, must keep the king at a distance from the adverse king, in order to be the better enabled to give perpetual check.

   **Black.** The queen gives check, at her king's knight's fourth square.

2. **White.** The queen covers the check.
   **Black.** The queen gives check, at her king's fourth square.

3. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's square.

   > He can no more give you check, without losing the game; but he may hinder your pawn from making a queen.

   **Black.** The queen, at her fourth square.

4. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's rook's second square.
   **Black.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's rook's square.

5. **White.** The queen covers the check.
   **Black.** The queen gives check, at the adverse king's fourth square.

6. **White.** The king, at the adverse king's knight's square.
   **Black.** The queen gives cheek, at the adverse king's knight's fourth square.

7. **White.** The queen covers the check.
   **Black.** The queen, at her king's third square. GAME Won with a QUEEN, against a Pawn near making a QUEEN.
