# Fourth Back-game of the Method of Playing

*At the 5th move of the Black.*

5. **White.** The queen, at her king's second square.
   **Black.** The king's knight, at the adverse king's knight's fourth square.

6. **White.** The queen's pawn, two moves.
   **Black.** The king's pawn takes the pawn.

7. **White.** The queen's bishop's pawn retakes the pawn
   **Black.** The king's bishop gives check.

8. **White.** The queen's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > If he takes this knight with his bishop, your game will be a great deal better, because this move would bring your pawns into the centre.

   **Black.** The king's knight, at his bishop's third square.

   > Here you might push your king's pawn one step, and by it get two moves; but this pawn once pushed, your adversary would offer you twice the exchange of the pawn, by pushing one step his queen's pawn, as well as that of his king's bishop. Your pawn would then remain alone and separate. It is better therefore to leave your two pawns in a front-line, to avoid the opening of your game. Miv

9. **White.** The king's bishop, at his queen's third square.
   **Black.** The king castles.

   > He castles, in order to attack your king's pawn with his rook: it is therefore absolutely necessary to you likewise to castle; so that, in case of an attack, you may sustain it with your king's rook.

10. **White.** The king castles.
   **Black.** The queen's pawn, one move.

11. **White.** The king's rook's pawn, one move.
   **Black.** The same.

12. **White.** The queen's bishop, at his queen's second square.

   > It is plain that the white are in the more desirable situation, having the advantage of three moves more than the black, and the pawns besides farther advanced. Nothing remains on the side of the white but to strive to disengage the king's bishop's pawn: that move would entirely decide the game in its own favour.

*A back-game: a variation resuming its parent game at move 5.*

Source: Philidor, *Analysis of the Game of Chess* (London, P. Elmsley, 1777), collated with volume I of the edition of 1790.
